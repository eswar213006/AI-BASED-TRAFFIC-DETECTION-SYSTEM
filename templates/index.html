Case Study & Computer Networks Project Documentation

Problem Statement

Traditional Rule-Based Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) rely on static signatures (SNORT rules). They fail to detect zero-day anomalies, modified attack vectors, and high-velocity network flow shifts. Signature upkeep is costly and inefficient in high-throughput enterprise networks.

Proposed AI Solution

An AI-Assisted Network Intrusion Detection System that converts continuous packet flows into statistical metric vectors (Packets/Sec, Bytes/Sec, Connection Duration, Failed Attempts) and utilizes a trained Random Forest Classifier to distinguish normal baseline traffic from malicious attacks with high confidence.

Academic Viva & Examination Q&A

Expected questions from Computer Networks professors with ideal CSE student responses:

Q1: What is the difference between Signature-based IDS and Anomaly-based IDS?

Answer: Signature-based IDS compares network packets against known patterns/signatures in a database (like anti-virus). It cannot detect new zero-day threats. Anomaly-based IDS (like our AI model) builds a baseline model of normal network traffic behavior and flags any statistically significant deviation as a potential intrusion.

Q2: Why did you choose Random Forest algorithm over simple Decision Trees or Logistic Regression?

Answer: Random Forest is an ensemble learning method that constructs multiple decision trees and merges their predictions via majority voting. It handles non-linear network feature correlations effectively, prevents overfitting, is resilient to noisy network telemetry, and provides out-of-the-box feature importance scores.

Q3: What key network features are most crucial for detecting DoS attacks vs Port Scans?

Answer: For DoS attacks, packet volume features like packets_per_second and bytes_per_second dominate. For Port Scanning, low total bytes per flow coupled with extremely short connection_duration and rapid sequential destination_port access patterns are key indicators.

Q4: How does your system calculate severity levels?

Answer: Severity is assigned based on threat potential: NORMAL flows receive LOW severity, Brute-Force authentication attacks receive MEDIUM severity, Port-Scan reconnaissance receives HIGH severity, and high-volume DoS floods that threaten availability receive CRITICAL severity.
